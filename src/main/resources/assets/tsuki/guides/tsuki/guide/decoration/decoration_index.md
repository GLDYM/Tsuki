# Building & Decoration

## Building Blocks

### Timber

Various types of wood can be obtained through [corresponding saplings](../crop/crop_index.md). Notably, bamboo blocks can be directly crafted into building materials, as well as fences and doors. Sunburnt bamboo blocks are obtained by exposing bamboo blocks to sunlight.

### Kawara<ItemImage id="tsuki:kawara" scale="0.6"/><ItemImage id="tsuki:kawara_block" scale="0.6"/><ItemImage id="tsuki:kawara_block_alter" scale="0.6"/>

Kawara are commonly used for traditional Japanese building roofs to protect from wind and rain. Through shapeless crafting, tile blocks can be converted to variants to change their texture direction.

<Recipe id="tsuki:kawara_block_to_alter" />

### Tatami<ItemImage id="tsuki:tatami" scale="0.6"/><ItemImage id="tsuki:tatami_sunburnt" scale="0.6"/><ItemImage id="tsuki:tatami_waxed" scale="0.6"/>

Tatami are the flooring material used in traditional Japanese rooms called Washitsu. Placing tatami in sunlight converts them to sunburnt variants. To prevent this conversion, you can wax tatami mats to create waxed variants.

### Straw Block<ItemImage id="tsuki:straw_block" scale="0.6"/>

Straw blocks are another common roof material that can be crafted from straw.

## Decorative Blocks

### Shoji<ItemImage id="tsuki:shoji" scale="0.6"/><ItemImage id="tsuki:shoji_1" scale="0.6"/><ItemImage id="tsuki:shoji_2" scale="0.6"/><ItemImage id="tsuki:shoji_3" scale="0.6"/><ItemImage id="tsuki:shoji_4" scale="0.6"/><ItemImage id="tsuki:shoji_5" scale="0.6"/>

Shoji are sliding screen doors used as room dividers in Japanese houses, available in latticed versions with wooden grid frames and smooth versions with paper.

### Noren<ItemImage id="tsuki:noren_blue" scale="0.6"/><ItemImage id="tsuki:noren_pink" scale="0.6"/><ItemImage id="tsuki:noren_white" scale="0.6"/>

Can be hung below blocks without blocking entity movement. Noren are door curtains commonly seen in Japanese bathhouses, inns, hot springs, and restaurants, often hung at doorways, windows, or between rooms for visual separation.

### Paper Lanterns<ItemImage id="tsuki:red_lantern" scale="0.6"/><ItemImage id="tsuki:white_lantern" scale="0.6"/>

Can be hung below blocks; brightness level 15 light source. Red lanterns are commonly hung under house eaves or at pub entrances of Izakaya, used for celebrations, while white lanterns are used for funerals.

### Stone Lanterns<ItemImage id="tsuki:stone_lantern" scale="0.6"/><ItemImage id="tsuki:cobblestone_lantern" scale="0.6"/><ItemImage id="tsuki:mossy_stone_lantern" scale="0.6"/>

Can be placed anywhere; brightness level 15 light source. Stone lanterns are commonly used along paths in gardens or around hot springs.

### Bamboo Lanterns<ItemImage id="tsuki:bamboo_lantern" scale="0.6"/>

Can be placed anywhere; brightness level 15 light source. Bamboo lanterns are commonly used above water surfaces

### Andon<ItemImage id="tsuki:andon" scale="0.6"/>

Can be placed on top of blocks; brightness level 15 light source. Andon are commonly used for yokai themes.

### Wind Chimes<ItemImage id="tsuki:windbell" scale="0.6"/>

Can be hung below blocks. Produces a tinkling sound when receiving random ticks.

### Kitunebi<ItemImage id="tsuki:kitunebi" scale="0.6"/>

Similar to vanilla light block; brightness level 15 light source. After placement, only players holding Kitunebi can see or break it.

### Taiko<ItemImage id="tsuki:taiko" scale="0.6"/>

Can be struck with a wooden stick while holding to produce sound.

### Obon<ItemImage id="tsuki:obon" scale="0.6"/>

Right-click while holding an item to place it in the obon and display it. The displayed item's facing matches the obon's facing. Can also be used to craft set meals.
